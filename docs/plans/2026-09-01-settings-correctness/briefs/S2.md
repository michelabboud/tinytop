## F1 — the configure path reads its own settings, under the lock

`configure_collector_if_changed` is `writer.rs:792-803`. Today it is
`async fn configure_collector_if_changed(state: &AppState, settings: &DashboardSettings)` and the caller supplies the settings.

Change it to `async fn configure_collector_if_changed(state: &AppState)`. Inside, in this exact order:

1. Acquire `state.collector_config` (as today, `:796`).
2. **Then** `state.store.get_settings().await`.
3. Compute `desired` via `collector_config_from`.
4. Return early if `applied.as_ref() == Some(&desired)` (the idempotence guard — **keep it**, ADR 0031 Consequences explains why it is now load-bearing rather than an optimisation).
5. Otherwise acquire `state.collector`, `configure`, and record `applied`.

The settings read must be **inside** the `collector_config` guard. That is the whole point: it makes the mutex the single serialization point, so two racing callers converge on the newest persisted row instead of one reverting the other. Do not "optimise" it back outside the lock.

**The function stays infallible** (returns `()`). A failed settings read logs once via `eprintln!` and returns without configuring; the next tick retries. It must not panic, must not fail an HTTP request, and must not abort startup.

Keep the existing lock-order comment at `:794-795` and **extend** it with one sentence saying why the read is inside the guard, citing ADR 0031. A future reader will otherwise try to hoist it.
